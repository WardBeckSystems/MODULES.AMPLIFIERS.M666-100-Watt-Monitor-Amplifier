## The Continuing History Of WBS.

_WBS INFO · Jan 30, 2011|44_

> not the 466, the M666 the devils power amp

— **kuzub**, Jan 30, 2011|44

> Actually the M460 vintage of modules were first plastic escutcheons. As we moved away from the M400 series heavy plastic, then aluminum front assemblies, Mr. Ward (Father Ward) Senior who ran the metal shoppe worked with engineering and developed the later adopted escutcheon and extrusion format that lasted well in to the ST days. The earlier modules were all plastic escutcheons, silk screened, and glued in to place. This was later dropped for aluminum escutcheons that were glued in place, then Erwin Schwalder and Father Ward developed the final phase of the assembly where the escutcheon was screwed in to place with 4 or 5 #440 pan head Phillips screws. The painted escutcheons were painted and backed to make the finish more-or-less permanent. As was the silk screening. I had the first hand experience with these as we were refurbishing 1970's vintage modules in building 1. Margaret League and the ladies would chisel off the old escutcheons, and glue on the new aluminum ones. THen later years they would again chisel off the aluminum ones and replace them with screw down ones. I was involved directly with this process many evenings, weekends and a couple of summers. As for the painting I spent a few years in the stock room, visited the vendors and learned of their various processes.
>
> As for the M666 - that started out as being a unit solely for Jimmy Fong and his crew, and for presenting consoles at the NAB. Yes some were sold to customers, but their initial purpose (as Lydia and I were discussing this very thing at breakfast this AM oddly enough) was so that when a WBS system was on display, the potential buyers saw nothing but WBS equipment. All made by us, designed by Dad, and used by us. The M666 was 1 of 2 true audio amplifyers that we made. I believe TOny - you have teh 20 or 50 watt units??

> CHAPTER 21

> Working from your massive question list Anthony, when I came to M466 I thought you meant the L/C module. I did question in my mind the "Bryston" question as I am familiar with them enough to know make audio power amps.
>
> So let's talk WBS monitor amps. This is a good time to mention rack mount gear color (colour) - GREEN vs GRAY. About the beginning of the "golden age" I refer to, 1970's & 1980's, around the early 1970's, front panels of rack mount gear - power supplies, monitor amps, DA's, IC frames, etc - went from a light "pea green" to a more neutral light grey. About this point the M606 disappeared completely. The M610 lasted for a few more production runs, but the M621 became the mainstay of WBS amps and was continually produced for over 20 years. The M666 also had as long a run, though not in as many units as the M621. The WBS customers were primarily BROADCASTERS, unlike Neve and others who started in the RECORDING industry and later moved to broadcasting. Canadian and US broadcasters have this "thing" for plug-in modular equipment, and also in general at that time did not believe that their monitor amps needed to approach kilowatt capability. The M621 and M666 were popular with WBS customers not only because they were made by WBS, but because they were "plug-in" units. Assuming a spare was nearby, they could be changed in seconds. CBC, ABC, and CBS consumed many of these units with their consoles. And yes, I still don't know of any WBS/Bryston link regarding the M666.
>
> An "aside" on the paint color for rack mount gear, sometime at the end of the 1960's or beginning of 1970's, CBS came to WBS for their first purchase, intercom gear. CBS "sooo" didn't like the pea green color (colour) that they provided WBS with their LGBK paint for the CBS front panels. Did this have anything to do with the subsequent change to gray at WBS?
